Choosing your pillow

Appui or Aplomb: how do I choose?

Both pillows answer the same problem — filling the gap between your head and the mattress so your neck stays in line with your back — with two opposite fillings. The Appui Pillow is contoured and filled with high-resilience fibre: soft, it springs back, can be re-plumped and adjusted a little by hand. The Aplomb Pillow is perforated, ventilated memory foam: it moulds to the neck, comes back slowly, and does not deform under the head. Both are 60 × 40 cm with removable covers. I sleep on my side. What height should I choose, and what if I have broad shoulders?

On your side, the gap to fill is the one between your ear and your shoulder: the broader the build, the more height you need. The Appui Pillow is 12 cm: that's the reference for a medium-to-broad build, or for a firm mattress the shoulder barely sinks into. The Aplomb Pillow offers two heights on the same pillow: the 12 cm bolster for side sleeping, the 10 cm one if your build is slight or your mattress soft, since the body sinks in further. When in doubt, take the higher one: you can make up for a slightly high pillow by sliding your shoulder forward, never for one that's too low.

I sleep on my back, on my front, or I keep changing position — what do you suggest?

On your back, the distance to fill is short: the Aplomb on its 10 cm side works, the low bolster sitting under the neck with the back of the skull resting in the hollow. On your front, neither of our pillows suits: that position calls for a very flat pillow, or none at all. If you change position several times a night, the Aplomb is the logical choice: its 10 and 12 cm bolsters let you go from back to side by turning your head, with nothing to readjust. The Appui still works if you mostly alternate side and back, provided you re-plump it when you switch.

I sweat at night. What do you suggest?

The Aplomb first: its foam is perforated right through and its cover is a breathable knit — two things that let air move instead of trapping it under your cheek. If you prefer the Appui, add the matching cooling pillowcase: its technical heat-dissipating knit draws heat away on contact instead of reflecting it back. These pillowcases exist for both models, Appui and Aplomb. Lastly, the covers come off and wash at 30 °C, which matters when you sweat: keep a spare pillowcase in rotation.

Can I use a PORTANCE pillow during pregnancy?

The Appui and the Aplomb are comfort pillows, not medical devices, and we give no medical advice. Nothing rules it out in principle, but your sleeping positions and your comfort change through the trimesters: that question belongs with your midwife or your doctor, not with us. If the pillow stops suiting you, the 30-night guarantee and the 14-day right of withdrawal apply as they do for any order.

Care

How do I wash the cover and the pillowcases?

Take the cover off, close its zip and wash at 30 °C, delicate cycle, with no bleach and no fabric softener: softener clogs the fibres, costs cotton its absorbency and technical knit its breathability. Air dry, laid flat, out of direct sun; a tumble dryer is not advised (shrinkage and loss of elasticity). The 200-thread combed cotton pillowcase can be ironed on a low setting; the cooling technical-knit pillowcases must not be ironed, as the iron's heat damages the yarn. Only put the cover back once it is completely dry.

Can the pillow itself be washed?

No, neither goes in a machine: that is exactly what the removable cover is for, taking the friction and the washing in its place. On the Appui (high-resilience fibre), treat a stain with a barely damp sponge and a little mild soap, dab without rubbing, then dry flat all the way through. On the Aplomb, water is memory foam's enemy: it lodges inside, escapes very badly and degrades the material — a barely damp cloth, never a soak. Air the bare pillow for an hour or two now and then, in the shade.

My pillow smells a little when I open it. Is that normal?

Yes, especially the Aplomb: memory foam that has been compressed and then packed gives off a new smell out of the box. Take the pillow out of its packaging and let it breathe flat, without its cover, in a ventilated room for 24 to 48 hours: the smell goes on its own. Don't try to speed it up with direct sun, a radiator or a hairdryer — heat damages the foam. If anything lingers beyond a few days, write to us at [TO BE COMPLETED].

Will the pillow go flat, and how long does it last?

Every pillow loses loft over time; what changes is the pace, depending on the filling. The Appui's high-resilience fibre packs down with use: shake it and re-plump it briefly each morning and it recovers its volume. The Aplomb's foam cannot be re-plumped: it keeps its shape by construction, but it ages and becomes slower to spring back. In daily use, a pillow of this kind is generally replaced every 2 to 3 years: the one that no longer recovers its shape by morning has done its time. Normal wear is not a defect; a manufacturing defect, on the other hand, falls under the 2-year statutory guarantee of conformity.

Sleep strips and nasal strips

What are sleep strips — and what are they not?

A PORTANCE sleep strip is a hypoallergenic adhesive strip you place on your lips before sleeping, to encourage breathing through the nose. Its central cut-out stays open: the mouth is not sealed, and you can speak, cough and breathe through your mouth if you need to. It is not a medical device: it cures nothing, treats nothing, prevents nothing, and replaces neither a treatment nor a healthcare professional's advice. PORTANCE nasal strips are a different product: spring strips stuck to the outside of the nose, mechanically holding the nostrils open. In both cases the effect is mechanical and a matter of comfort, nothing more.

How do I apply a strip, and for how long each night?

Put it on just before bed, on clean, dry lips: no balm, no cream, no oil, or it won't hold. Centre it horizontally on closed lips without stretching the skin, press for five seconds, and leave the central cut-out clear. Start with an hour or two in the evening, awake, to see how you get on with it before keeping it for a whole night; after that it is worn for the duration of sleep and removed on waking. To take it off, dampen the edges with warm water if you feel resistance and peel slowly, never in one sharp pull. Sleep strips come in 1, 3 and 6-month packs, and nasal strips in 1 and 3-month packs.

When should sleep strips not be used?

Do not use sleep strips, and talk to a healthcare professional at the slightest doubt, in the following situations:

  • suspected, undiagnosed or untreated sleep apnoea;
  • a blocked nose or nasal obstruction: a cold, allergy, sinusitis, a deviated septum;
  • respiratory conditions (asthma in particular) or cardiac conditions;
  • alcohol, sedatives, sleeping pills or muscle relaxants in the evening;
  • nausea, vomiting or gastro-oesophageal reflux;
  • children and adolescents;
  • pregnancy;
  • irritated, broken or allergic skin around the mouth;
  • anyone who could not remove the strip themselves during the night.

Remove it immediately in case of discomfort, breathlessness, nausea or irritation, and do not go back to it without medical advice.

I have sensitive skin, or a beard. Can I use them?

The adhesive is hypoallergenic, but no adhesive is neutral on every skin. Test a strip on your forearm for a few hours first: if redness appears and stays after removal, go no further. Also avoid if the skin around your lips is chapped, irritated, broken or freshly waxed, or if you are using a topical treatment on that area. On a full beard or moustache, adhesion is unreliable and removal pulls on the hair: the strip is designed for skin and lips, not for facial hair.

Can a child or a teenager use them?

No. PORTANCE sleep strips are for adults only: we do not recommend them for children or adolescents, and they should be stored out of their reach. If a child's night-time breathing worries you, that is a matter for a paediatrician or a family doctor, not for a comfort product. The same reservation applies to anyone who could not peel the strip off themselves during the night.
